# Complex Collective — Brand Guidelines

> This document defines the brand identity, visual system, voice, and principles for Complex Collective. It is designed to be specific enough that any contractor, designer, or developer can produce on-brand work without guessing.

---

## 1. Brand Foundation

### Who We Are

Complex Collective is a premium YouTube growth agency for coaches and consultants doing $50k+/month. We don't run ads. We don't do social media management. We build YouTube ecosystems that turn our clients into the undeniable authority in their niche — so their customers find *them*.

### Core Promise

**We make you the authority your market can't ignore.**

YouTube is the only channel where a single piece of content can generate leads for years without ongoing spend. We build the strategy, systems, and execution that make that happen — so our clients stop chasing and start attracting.

### Brand Pillars

| Pillar | What It Means |
|--------|--------------|
| **Bold Execution** | We don't play it safe. We test, we push, we try what others won't — but always with strategic intent behind it. |
| **Premium by Design** | We charge premium prices because we deliver premium outcomes. Every touchpoint — from our site to our deliverables — should signal "this costs money for a reason." |
| **Long-Game Thinking** | YouTube isn't a quick fix. We work with clients who think in 1–5 year time horizons. We attract patient, ambitious operators — not desperate lead-chasers. |
| **Proof Over Promise** | We lead with results, not hype. Post-execution is our differentiator. We show what we built, not what we *could* build. |
| **Hands-Off Growth** | Our clients film. We handle the rest. The value prop is an acquisition channel that compounds without constant input. |

### Positioning Statement

For coaches and consultants doing $50k+/month who are tired of acquisition channels that die when they stop feeding them — Complex Collective builds YouTube presences that compound over time, turning clients into the go-to authority in their niche. Unlike ad agencies and social media managers, we build an asset that works harder the longer it runs.

## 3. Color System

### Philosophy

Dark-mode dominant. The palette communicates authority, premium positioning, and confidence. The dark foundation makes accent colors hit harder and signals sophistication over friendliness.

### Primary Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|------|-------|-----|-----|-------|
| **Deep Space** (Primary Dark) | Near-black navy | `#0D0F1A` | 13, 15, 26 | Page backgrounds, primary surfaces |
| **Midnight** (Secondary Dark) | Dark navy | `#161829` | 22, 24, 41 | Cards, elevated surfaces, nav bars |
| **Slate** (Tertiary Dark) | Muted navy | `#1E2035` | 30, 32, 53 | Borders, dividers, subtle depth layers |

### Accent Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|------|-------|-----|-----|-------|
| **Electric Violet** (Primary Accent) | Deep electric purple | `#8B5CF6` | 139, 92, 246 | CTAs, key highlights, interactive elements, logo accent |
| **Vivid Violet** (Hover/Active) | Brighter purple | `#A78BFA` | 167, 139, 250 | Hover states, secondary highlights |
| **Royal Blue** (Secondary Accent) | Bold blue | `#3B82F6` | 59, 130, 246 | Links, secondary CTAs, data/stats, checkmark accent |

### Neutral Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|------|-------|-----|-----|-------|
| **White** | Pure white | `#FFFFFF` | 255, 255, 255 | Headlines, primary text on dark backgrounds |
| **Silver** | Light gray | `#C4C7D4` | 196, 199, 212 | Body text, descriptions |
| **Muted** | Medium gray | `#6B7094` | 107, 112, 148 | Captions, labels, secondary info |
| **Dim** | Dark gray | `#3A3D52` | 58, 61, 82 | Disabled states, placeholder text |

### Gradient (Use Sparingly)

```
Primary Gradient: linear-gradient(135deg, #8B5CF6 0%, #3B82F6 100%)
```

Use only for hero accents, key CTA buttons, or section dividers. Never for body text or large surface fills.

### Color Rules

1. **Dark backgrounds are the default.** Light mode is not part of the brand.
2. **Electric Violet is the signature.** It should appear on every page but never dominate — it's the accent, not the base.
3. **White text on dark backgrounds only.** Never use dark text on light backgrounds in marketing materials.
4. **The gradient is a premium touch.** Use it for the most important CTA on a page. One per viewport max.
5. **No additional colors.** If you need a success/error/warning state, use green `#22C55E`, amber `#F59E0B`, red `#EF4444` — standard utility colors only.

### Comparison: Current vs. Recommended Purple

| | Current (Orchid) | Recommended (Electric Violet) |
|--|---|---|
| **Hex** | ~`#C56CF0` | `#8B5CF6` |
| **Feel** | Playful, soft, approachable | Bold, electric, premium |
| **On dark bg** | Slightly washed out | High contrast, commanding |
| **Signal** | Creative, casual | Authority, confidence |

The shift is subtle but meaningful — moving from a pink-leaning orchid to a deeper, blue-leaning violet that reads as more serious money.

## 4. Typography

### Philosophy

Bold and commanding for headlines. Clean and readable for body. Every type choice should feel like it was made by someone who charges $4k/month — not a Canva template.

### Type Scale

| Role | Font | Weight | Size (Desktop) | Size (Mobile) | Line Height | Letter Spacing |
|------|------|--------|----------------|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| **H1 — Hero** | Inter | 800 (ExtraBold) | 64–80px | 36–44px | 1.05 | -0.03em |
| **H2 — Section** | Inter | 700 (Bold) | 44–56px | 28–36px | 1.1 | -0.02em |
| **H3 — Subsection** | Inter | 600 (SemiBold) | 28–32px | 22–26px | 1.2 | -0.01em |
| **Body Large** | Inter | 400 (Regular) | 18–20px | 16–18px | 1.6 | 0 |
| **Body** | Inter | 400 (Regular) | 16px | 15px | 1.6 | 0 |
| **Caption/Label** | Inter | 500 (Medium) | 13–14px | 12–13px | 1.4 | 0.04em |
| **Eyebrow** | Inter | 600 (SemiBold) | 12–14px | 11–13px | 1.4 | 0.12em |
| **CTA Button** | Inter | 600 (SemiBold) | 16px | 15px | 1 | 0.02em |

### Font: Inter

**Why Inter:** It's a workhorse that handles both bold headlines and clean body copy. At heavy weights (700–800), it has the punch of a display font. At regular weights, it's effortlessly readable. It's free, web-optimized, and universally supported.

**Load these weights only:** 400, 500, 600, 700, 800

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Inter:wght@400;500;600;700;800&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```

### Typography Rules

1. **Headlines are always white (`#FFFFFF`) on dark backgrounds.** No exceptions.
2. **Eyebrow text is always uppercase, Electric Violet (`#8B5CF6`), tracked wide.** This is a key brand pattern.
3. **Body text uses Silver (`#C4C7D4`).** Never pure white — it's too harsh for paragraphs.
4. **Tight letter-spacing on headlines, loose on eyebrows.** This contrast creates visual hierarchy.
5. **Never use light/thin weights (100–300).** The brand is bold, not delicate.
6. **Minimum body text size: 15px.** Anything smaller fails the premium test.

### Alternative Display Font (Optional)

If you want extra punch on hero headlines or key marketing moments:

**Clash Display** (by Indian Type Foundry, available on Fontshare — free for commercial use)

Use at weight 600–700 for H1 hero headlines only. Everything else stays Inter. This creates a clear hierarchy: Clash for the statement, Inter for everything else.

## 5. Logo System

### Current Logo Elements

The existing Complex Collective logo contains:

- **Wordmark:** "Cmplex" in dark navy, geometric sans-serif
- **C Icon:** The standalone "C" with a purple circle/ring inside it (eye/lens motif)
- **X Accent:** Blue checkmark/arrow element on the "x"
- **Missing "o":** The purple circle in the C conceptually replaces the "o" — clever but may cause readability issues at small sizes

### Logo Usage on Dark Backgrounds

When the brand shifts to dark-mode, the logo needs to be inverted:
- **Wordmark:** White (`#FFFFFF`) instead of dark navy
- **C Icon Purple:** Electric Violet (`#8B5CF6`) instead of orchid
- **X Accent:** Royal Blue (`#3B82F6`)

### Logo Clear Space

Minimum clear space around the logo = the height of the lowercase "m" in the wordmark, on all sides.

### Logo Don'ts

- Never place the logo on a busy/patterned background without a solid backing
- Never stretch, rotate, or skew the logo
- Never change the logo colors outside the approved palette
- Never use the logo smaller than 120px wide (wordmark) or 32px (icon)

### Logo Recommendation for Redesign

The current logo has strong bones — the C-with-circle motif is distinctive. For the redesign, consider:

1. **Spell out "Complex Collective"** — the abbreviated "Cmplex" sacrifices clarity for cleverness. At this price point, clarity wins.
2. **Keep the C icon** — it works as a favicon, app icon, and social avatar
3. **Unify the accent colors** — using both purple AND blue in the wordmark creates split attention. Pick one (Electric Violet) for the icon, let the wordmark be clean white/dark
4. **Consider a tagline lockup** — "Complex Collective" over a smaller "YouTube Growth Agency" gives instant clarity

## 6. Brand Voice & Tone

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What It Sounds Like | What It Doesn't Sound Like |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Direct** | "Here's what we do. Here's what it costs. Here's what you get." | Hedging, over-qualifying, burying the point |
| **Confident** | "We build YouTube channels that generate leads for years." | "We *might* be able to help you *potentially* grow..." |
| **Honest** | "YouTube takes 6–12 months to gain real traction. If you need leads tomorrow, we're not the right fit." | Overpromising timelines, hiding the hard parts |
| **Premium** | Concise. No filler. Every sentence earns its place. | Walls of text, excessive exclamation marks, buzzword soup |
| **Provocative** | Challenges assumptions. "Your ad spend is a subscription. Your YouTube channel is an asset." | Safe, generic, forgettable |

### Tone Matrix

| Context | Tone Adjustment | Example |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| **Landing page hero** | Bold, provocative, concise | "Stop renting attention. Own it." |
| **Service descriptions** | Clear, specific, outcome-focused | "We handle strategy, scripting, editing, and optimization. You show up and film." |
| **Sales calls** | Straight-shooter, consultative | "Based on your niche and current content, here's what I'd do differently." |
| **Social media** | Punchy, opinionated, educational | "Hot take: If your YouTube strategy is 'post and pray,' you don't have a strategy." |
| **Email sequences** | Warm but direct, value-first | No fluff intros. Lead with the insight, close with the ask. |
| **Case studies** | Data-driven, let results speak | "Channel grew from 200 to 14,000 subscribers. More importantly: 47 qualified leads/month from YouTube alone." |

### Copy Rules

1. **Lead with outcomes, not process.** Nobody cares about your "proprietary framework" — they care about leads.
2. **Use contrast to make points.** "Most agencies ___. We ___." This structure is a brand staple.
3. **Short paragraphs.** Max 3 sentences. If you can say it in one, do.
4. **No corporate jargon.** "Synergy," "leverage," "holistic" — kill them all.
5. **Numbers over adjectives.** "47 leads/month" beats "significant growth" every time.
6. **Talk to one person.** "You" not "our clients." Direct address always.

### Words We Use

| Use | Instead Of |
|-----|-----------|
| Authority | Thought leader |
| Asset | Content |
| Compound | Scale |
| Build | Create |
| Attract | Generate (when talking about leads finding the client) |
| Investment | Cost/price (when talking about our services) |

### Words We Never Use

- "Guru" / "Ninja" / "Rockstar"
- "Crushing it" / "Killing it"
- "Game-changer" / "Disruptive"
- "Guaranteed results"
- "Viral"
- "Hack" / "Trick"

## 7. Imagery & Visual Style

### Photography Direction

- **Dark, moody, high-contrast.** Think studio lighting, not candid iPhone shots.
- **People over objects.** Show coaches/consultants in their element — recording, speaking, leading.
- **No stock photo energy.** If it looks like it came from Shutterstock, it's wrong.
- **Desaturated backgrounds, saturated subjects.** The person pops, the environment recedes.

### Iconography

- **Line icons only.** 1.5–2px stroke weight. Rounded caps.
- **Monochrome (Silver `#C4C7D4`) by default.** Electric Violet on hover/active.
- **Simple and geometric.** No illustrated or 3D icons.

### UI Elements

| Element | Style |
|---------|-------|
| **Cards** | `background: #161829`, `border: 1px solid #1E2035`, `border-radius: 12px` |
| **Buttons (Primary)** | `background: linear-gradient(135deg, #8B5CF6, #3B82F6)`, `color: #FFFFFF`, `border-radius: 8px`, `padding: 14px 28px` |
| **Buttons (Secondary)** | `background: transparent`, `border: 1px solid #8B5CF6`, `color: #8B5CF6`, `border-radius: 8px` |
| **Buttons (Ghost)** | `background: transparent`, `color: #C4C7D4`, underline on hover |
| **Inputs** | `background: #1E2035`, `border: 1px solid #3A3D52`, `color: #FFFFFF`, `border-radius: 8px` |
| **Dividers** | `border-top: 1px solid #1E2035` |
| **Hover states** | Accent elements shift from `#8B5CF6` to `#A78BFA`. Cards get `border-color: #8B5CF6` with subtle glow. |

### Motion & Animation

- **Subtle and purposeful.** Fade-ups on scroll (200–400ms, ease-out). No bouncing, no spinning.
- **Transitions: 200ms ease-out** for hover states. 300ms for section reveals.
- **No autoplay video backgrounds** unless they're ambient/atmospheric and muted.
- **Parallax is acceptable** but keep it minimal — 10–20% offset max.

## 8. Landing Page Structure (Recommended)

Based on the brand guidelines above, the new Complex Collective landing page should follow this structure:

### Section Flow

1. **Nav** — Logo (white version) + minimal links + "Book a Call" CTA (gradient button)
2. **Hero** — Bold headline, 1-line subhead, single CTA. No video background. Clean, type-driven.
3. **Problem/Agitation** — Call out the frustration: "Every channel you're using right now dies when you stop feeding it."
4. **Solution** — What Complex Collective does, in 3 bullet points max.
5. **How It Works** — 3-step visual: Strategy → Film → We Handle the Rest
6. **Results/Proof** — Stats, client outcomes, specific numbers. No vague testimonials.
7. **Who This Is For** — Qualifying section. $50k+/mo, existing channel, long-term mindset.
8. **About/Founder** — Jeffrey's story. Brief, credibility-building, personal.
9. **FAQ** — Handle objections: timeline, pricing, what's included, what they need to do.
10. **Final CTA** — Calendly embed or link. Repeat the core promise.
11. **Footer** — Minimal. Logo, links, legal.

### Key Design Principles for the Page

- **Above the fold: headline + CTA. Nothing else competes.**
- **One CTA per section max.** Don't give them 5 things to click.
- **Whitespace is premium.** Generous padding (80–120px between sections).
- **Sections alternate between `#0D0F1A` and `#161829`** for visual rhythm.
- **Stats use large type (48–64px)** in Electric Violet or White.
